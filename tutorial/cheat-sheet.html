Make the emails personal

5 min

In Lovable

  1. Copy the text below
  2. Paste it into Lovable’s chat and send
Paste into Lovable
Open supabase/seed-blocks.sql and fill in the block bodies. Write them in my
voice, using the exact profile and challenge wording already in
src/funnel.config.ts. Do not change any other file.

For EACH of my 6 profiles, write PROFILE_MIRROR: two or three short lines
describing that person's actual day back to them. No advice, no pitch.

For EACH of my 6 profiles, write CTA_LINE: one line on why turning up matters
for that specific person.

For EACH of my 5 challenges, write PAIN_TWIST: name the struggle, then go one
level under it to the thing behind it, and reframe it as a structural problem
rather than a personal failing.

For EACH of my 5 challenges, write PROOF: two or three lines of reasoning for
why that struggle happens. No statistics I cannot source, no case studies, no
client results. Explanation only.

Rules:
- Write like I am writing to one friend, not to a list.
- Plain sentences. Short lines. No exclamation marks, no hype words.
- Never invent a number, a client, or a result.
- Do not give away any session's reveal.
- Replace every NEEDS-COPY. Tell me if any are left.
This is the whole point of the email engine. Everyone picked a situation and a struggle when they registered. Six situations by five struggles means 30 versions of every email, from one piece of writing.

Write the call script

5 min

On bolna.ai

Your agent script
You are calling someone who signed up for a free online session.

Say this, naturally, and keep it under twenty seconds:

"Hi, is that [first name]? This is a quick reminder from [YOUR NAME]'s team.
Your free online session starts in about thirty minutes. You'll find the
joining link in your email. Will you be able to join us?"

If they say yes: "Great, see you there." End the call.
If they say no: "No problem, thanks for letting me know." End the call.
If they ask something you cannot answer: "I'll have someone email you about
that." End the call.

Never make promises about results, money, or outcomes.
Never pressure anyone. If they want to go, let them go.
Voice providers check your script, and they are strict. Scripts get rejected for words that are harmless in context. Avoid anything sounding like a money guarantee, anything about earnings, and unverifiable claims like “the top 3%”. If your brand name itself sounds dramatic, use your event name instead. Plain and boring gets approved. Clever does not.
10

Connect it

3 min

In your Control Room

  1. Open yourdomain.com/control-room
  2. Click “Email me a code”, then enter it
  3. Go to the Settings tab
  4. Fill in these five bolna_enabled → true
    bolna_agent_id → your Agent ID
    bolna_api_key → your API key
    bolna_lead_time_minutes → 30
    bolna_default_country_code → e.g. +91
  5. Click away from each field to save it
Your key is safe. Stored where only the backend can read it, never in a file, never anywhere a visitor’s browser can reach.

2 min

On your own site

  1. Register on your funnel with your real number
  2. Wait for the callThirty minutes before your event. To test sooner, temporarily set the event date to today.

Part 3

Get people to it

A funnel with no traffic is a website. Two routes: paid and free. Start free, add paid once you know the funnel converts.

12

Prove the pixel is working

10 min

Meta · then your site

Do this before spending anything. An ad running against a broken pixel spends real money and learns nothing.

  1. Go to business.facebook.com/events_manager
  2. Connect data source → Web → name it
  3. Copy the pixel IDThe long number.
  4. Paste this into Lovable Set tracking.metaPixelId in src/funnel.config.ts to [your ID]. Change nothing else.
  5. Back in Events Manager, open “Test Events”
  6. Enter your site address and register on your own funnel
  7. Watch for PageView, then LeadIf Lead never appears, stop and fix it. That is the event every ad will optimise towards.
Advanced matching is already built in. Registrations are matched to real people rather than guessed at, which is most of the difference between ads that find your buyers and ads that do not.
13

Free traffic first

Ongoing

Wherever you already have people

Your first fifty registrations should cost nothing. They tell you whether the funnel converts before you pay to find out.

  1. Put the link everywhere you already are Instagram bio, YouTube descriptions, LinkedIn, email signature, WhatsApp status.
  2. Message people individually, not in bulk Twenty personal messages beat one broadcast to a thousand. It is slower and it works.
  3. Post the problem, not the event Talk about the thing your audience is stuck on. The link goes at the end, once, without a pitch.
  4. Ask each registrant to bring one person Referral tracking is already built in, so you can see who did.
  5. Watch the Control Room daily Which day, which source, how many. Patterns show up faster than you expect.
Aim for 50 registrations before you pay for any. If a warm audience will not register free, a cold one will not register paid. Fix the page first.
14

Then paid ads

30 min to set up

Meta Ads Manager

  1. Create a campaign, objective: LeadsNot Traffic. Traffic buys clicks. Leads buys registrations, and that is what you are counting.
  2. Set the conversion event to LeadThe one you watched fire in step 12.
  3. Start with one audience and a small daily budget Broad targeting in your country, your age range. Let the pixel find people. Narrow targeting starves it of data.
  4. Write three different hooks, same offer One naming the pain, one naming the outcome, one asking a question. You are testing which problem people react to.
  5. Leave it alone for three daysEditing daily resets the learning and wastes the spend that bought it.
  6. Then judge on cost per registration Not on clicks, not on reach. Registrations, and how many showed up.
The number that matters is show-up rate, not registrations. A hundred registrations with fifteen attending is worse than forty with twenty-five. Everything in Part 2 exists for that number.
